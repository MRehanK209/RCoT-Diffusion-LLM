\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Autoregressive language models are the dominant interface for contemporary chain-of-thought reasoning and planning. They generate left to right: each token is sampled conditioned on an already committed prefix, and later tokens cannot directly revise earlier choices. This interface is simple, scalable, and highly effective, but reasoning and planning tasks often require global consistency, correction of earlier assumptions, and exploration of multiple candidate traces rather than a single irreversible continuation \cite{vaswani2017attention,brown2020language,wei2022chain,wang2023selfconsistency}.

Diffusion language models offer a plausible alternative evaluation target because they generate through masked or iterative denoising rather than strict next-token commitment. A diffusion model can refine a partially specified sequence or block over multiple steps, so the original motivation for this thesis was that such models might produce more coherent low-budget reasoning traces and might be useful in ensemble-style reasoning where several candidate traces are generated and selected \cite{austin2021structured,nie2025llada,ye2025dream}. This is a motivation, not a superiority claim: iterative refinement could help first-sample planning, but repeated autoregressive sampling could still explore a broader set of candidate solutions.

The thesis therefore asks a protocol-dependent question rather than a one-sided one: do diffusion LLMs and autoregressive LLMs differ in how they trade off first-sample accuracy, sampling diversity, pass@k growth, and per-question failure modes on reasoning and planning tasks? To answer this, the study compares LLaDA, Dream, Qwen2.5, and Llama~3.1 across GSM8K, Countdown-cd4, and Trip Planning under deterministic accuracy, stochastic pass@k, parser-controlled scoring, prompt-mode ablations, and per-question correlation analysis.

The empirical result is not that diffusion language models generally replace autoregressive models. On the planning-oriented tasks, diffusion models often produce stronger deterministic or low-budget solutions, while autoregressive models benefit more from large-$k$ sampling and can overtake at higher sample budgets. GSM8K is saturated at high $k$ and is most useful here as a prompt-sensitivity and parser-sensitivity sanity check. The ranking therefore depends on whether the evaluation asks for one strong answer, searches over many stochastic candidates, or penalizes deviations from a task-specific output format.

\paragraph{Contributions.} The thesis makes three contributions. First, it provides a controlled comparison of LLaDA, Dream, Qwen2.5, and Llama~3.1 at similar model scale across three reasoning and planning benchmarks, preceded by a diffusion hyperparameter sweep that characterizes the generation settings used in the main comparison \cite{nie2025llada,ye2025dream,qwen2024qwen25,grattafiori2024llama,cobbe2021gsm8k,xie2024travelplanner}. Second, it reports a protocol-dependent ranking pattern: diffusion models are stronger in deterministic or low-$k$ planning settings, while autoregressive models gain more from stochastic sampling and can overtake at high pass@k. Third, it adds parser-sensitivity, sampling-diversity, per-question correlation, and oracle-union analyses to show that the paradigms solve overlapping but non-identical subsets of problems. The oracle analysis is treated only as an upper bound on complementarity, not as a deployable ensemble method.
