\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments show that the comparison between diffusion and autoregressive language models depends strongly on the evaluation regime. A single deterministic answer, a low-$k$ sample set, and a high-$k$ pass@k score reward different behavior. This distinction matters most on the planning tasks, where a correct output must satisfy several constraints at once.

The low-budget results support the motivation for studying diffusion models. On Countdown-cd4 base, LLaDA-Base and Dream-Base have the strongest pass@1 scores. On Trip Planning, Dream variants occupy the top of the deterministic configuration ranking. These results suggest that iterative denoising can be competitive when the evaluation gives the model only one or a few chances to produce a valid plan.

The high-budget results show the other side of the trade-off. Autoregressive models can benefit substantially from repeated stochastic sampling. On Countdown Base, Qwen-Base rises from pass@1$=0.040$ to pass@128$=0.513$, while LLaDA-Base rises from $0.143$ to $0.272$. The ranking therefore reverses as the sampling budget increases. In Trip Planning, the pattern is less clean because parser failures and prompt format matter more, but the strongest high-$k$ curve again comes from an autoregressive model under the tested settings.

These findings argue against a single paradigm-level ranking. When only a small number of candidates can be generated, the evaluated planning tasks give a reason to consider diffusion models. When many candidates can be sampled and checked, autoregressive models may recover more through test-time search. GSM8K illustrates a third case: when a benchmark saturates at high $k$, it becomes more useful for diagnosing prompt and parser sensitivity than for separating generation paradigms.

The per-question analyses support a complementary interpretation. The models do not fail on identical sets of questions, and the oracle unions in Section~\ref{sec:results-correlation} show that additional correct samples exist across paradigms. A practical follow-up would need a verifier, selector, router, or parser-aware repair mechanism that can choose among candidates without access to the gold answer.
