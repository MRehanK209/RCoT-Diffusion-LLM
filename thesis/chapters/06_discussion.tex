\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the empirical results say}

The benchmark results form a single story when read in order. On constrained arithmetic planning, diffusion language models are the stronger low-$k$ models: LLaDA-Base reaches Countdown pass@1$=0.143$ versus Qwen-Base $0.040$, a $3.5\times$ gap. On natural-language planning, Dream-Base leads Trip Planning at every $k\le 16$. On grade-school math, the diffusion advantage is absent at base scale (Dream-Base $0.724$ versus Qwen-Base $0.709$), because the benchmark saturates before the paradigms can be separated.

As the sampling budget grows, the ranking reverses on both planning benchmarks. The strongest model at Countdown pass@1 (LLaDA, $0.143$) becomes the weakest at pass@128 ($0.272$), while the weakest at pass@1 (Llama, $0.027$) reaches $0.495$. The same pattern holds in weaker form on Trip Planning. The reversal is absent on saturated GSM8K simply because there is no headroom left. The ranking that the evaluator reports therefore depends on the sampling budget, not just on the models.

Prompt mode and few-shot count are first-class experimental variables, not nuisance factors. The largest single effect in the entire thesis appears here: removing the chat template from Llama-Instruct on GSM8K drops pass@1 from $0.782$ to $0.211$, a $57$-point swing on a benchmark where both prompt modes converge to pass@128$=1.000$. Diffusion instruct models are more robust to flattening, while autoregressive instruct models gain heavily from few-shot examples (Qwen-Instruct moves from $0.534$ at 0-shot to $0.789$ at 4-shot).

Per-question correlation analysis adds a structural finding that the aggregate pass@k tables cannot show. On Countdown Base, LLaDA-Dream Pearson $r=0.638$ exceeds Dream-Qwen $r=0.572$, despite Dream's Qwen lineage. On Trip Planning Instruct, all cross-paradigm correlations fall in $[0.16, 0.25]$ while AR-AR is $0.719$. Generation paradigm is therefore a stronger predictor of which questions a model fails on than shared pretrained architecture, at least on these planning tasks.

Parser layer behavior is benchmark-specific. GSM8K and Countdown parser-crash rates are below $1\%$; format compliance is the issue. Trip Planning is parser-heavy: $54\%$ of Llama-Instruct templated samples produce unparseable plans, and any pass@k claim on this benchmark must be read together with the parser-failure rates in Section~\ref{sec:parser-results}.

\subsection{Mechanistic interpretation: concentration versus diversity}

The diversity metrics in Section~\ref{sec:results-diversity} make the concentration-versus-diversity reading more concrete. On Countdown base, LLaDA averages $1.31$ unique normalized answers per question against Qwen's $44.65$. The Pearson correlation result strengthens this picture: diffusion models share failure modes with one another more than they share with the autoregressive model whose backbone Dream inherits. Together, these observations are consistent with --- but do not directly prove --- a mechanism in which iterative denoising concentrates probability mass around a small number of globally coherent solutions, while autoregressive temperature sampling explores more diverse local trajectories.

The hypothesis is falsifiable. It predicts that increasing diffusion sampling temperature, reducing block length, or extending denoising steps should shift dLLM behavior toward more AR-like sample diversity and steeper pass@k growth. Section~\ref{sec:results-hyperparameters} partially tests this for Dream and finds the prediction does not hold under fast-dLLM: step count is effectively a no-op, suggesting that fast-dLLM's parallel decoding already collapses Dream's denoising into a step-invariant operation. A direct test on standard dLLM, or on temperature variation, is left to future work.

\subsection{What changes if the evaluation protocol changes}

The single most important methodological message is that the answer to the question ``which paradigm is better?'' depends entirely on what the evaluator measures. Deterministic accuracy ranks dLLMs above AR on planning tasks. Pass@1 with $n>1$ keeps the same ranking. Pass@128 reverses it. Few-shot examples close almost all of the zero-shot gaps. Flat versus templated prompting can move a single model by tens of points. Future work in this area should report at least three points on the pass@k curve, both prompt formats for instruct models, and parser-failure rates as a sanity check before claiming any paradigm-level result.

\subsection{Implications for ensemble and hybrid systems}

The correlation and oracle analyses point toward complementarity rather than redundancy. On Trip Planning instruct, cross-paradigm correlations fall in $[0.16, 0.25]$ and the Dream$+$Llama union improves over the best single model by $6.5$ percentage points. A practical ensemble that recovers even half of this oracle ceiling would be a clear improvement over any single-paradigm system. The thesis does not implement such a selector, so the reported gain is necessarily an upper bound, but the gap between best-single and best-union is large enough on planning benchmarks to motivate a follow-up project on selective routing or combiner training.
