\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The results support a consistent but carefully limited theory: diffusion LLMs appear to concentrate probability mass through iterative denoising, giving them stronger deterministic and low-$k$ planning behavior, while autoregressive models appear to maintain broader sampling diversity and therefore improve faster under pass@k. The new diversity metrics make that reading more defensible, especially on Countdown-cd4, where diffusion models emit very few distinct normalized answers per question and AR models emit many more. Even so, the evidence is only \emph{consistent with} this mechanism; it does not directly observe internal probability mass.

The thesis therefore rejects a single global answer to the question ``which paradigm is better?'' Diffusion models are better when the evaluation permits only one or a few samples, especially on constrained planning. Autoregressive models are better when the evaluation permits many samples and a parser can search over them. The scientific conclusion depends directly on the evaluation protocol.

Prompting is also part of the result rather than a nuisance variable. GSM8K zero-shot instruct favors the diffusion models, but 4-shot prompting lets Qwen and Llama catch up. Countdown and Trip Planning show that flat versus templated prompting can move individual models substantially, sometimes enough to alter qualitative claims. The implication is methodological: prompt mode, chat templating, and few-shot count should be reported alongside model family and backend.

The parser analysis adds a more sobering lesson. GSM8K and Countdown are not dominated by parser crashes, but GSM8K scores still depend on a strict boxed-answer convention and Trip Planning scores can be dominated by malformed plans. Parser dependence is therefore a real threat to validity, not a footnote. The canonical thesis numbers should stay tied to the official evaluation path, but any cross-paradigm claim should be read together with the parser-sensitivity appendix.

The correlation and oracle analyses strengthen the complementarity claim. On Countdown Base, diffusion models fail and succeed together more than Dream and Qwen do, despite the shared Dream/Qwen lineage. On Trip Planning Instruct, cross-paradigm correlations are low and the Dream+Llama oracle union improves over the best single model by 6.5 percentage points. This creates a natural future direction: hybrid evaluation or ensemble methods that exploit the different failure modes rather than forcing a single winner.
