\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results are organized around the main distinctions introduced in the evaluation design. The chapter first reports the diffusion hyperparameter sweep, because generation length, denoising steps, and block length determine the operating regime for LLaDA and Dream. It then uses GSM8K as a diagnostic benchmark for prompt format and answer extraction, Countdown-cd4 as the clearest case of a budget-dependent ranking reversal, and Trip Planning as a structured natural-language planning task. The final sections examine parser failures, sampling gains, answer diversity, per-question correlations, and oracle-union complementarity.

The pass@k question from Chapters~\ref{sec:introduction} and~\ref{sec:model-background} is whether LLaDA and Dream show a large-$k$ coverage effect analogous to the reversal reported in prior RLVR work: a model can be weaker at estimated stochastic pass@1 but cover more correct solutions when many samples are drawn \cite{yue2025rlvr}. Across the tested configurations, the experiments do not show such a large-$k$ advantage for the diffusion runs. Instead, LLaDA and Dream are competitive or leading in several low-budget planning settings, while Qwen and Llama usually gain more from repeated stochastic sampling.

Tables~\ref{tab:main-results-gsm8k}--\ref{tab:main-results-trip} in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-main-stochastic-results} give the main stochastic results, including bootstrap confidence intervals and primary-parser-entry failure rates.
