\section{Model Background and Thesis Hypothesis}
\label{sec:model-background}

\subsection{Autoregressive Generation}
For an autoregressive model, a sequence $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$ is modeled as
\begin{equation}
    p(x)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p(x_t \mid x_{<t}).
\end{equation}
Generation proceeds left to right. Greedy decoding chooses the most likely next token at every step, while temperature sampling draws from a softened or sharpened distribution. Repeated stochastic sampling can therefore explore many different reasoning traces. This gives autoregressive models a natural route to high pass@k performance when many attempts are affordable: each sample may follow a different path through the solution space.

\subsection{Masked Diffusion Generation}
Masked diffusion language models begin from a masked or partially masked sequence and iteratively refine tokens over a fixed number of diffusion steps. The practical generation configuration includes generation length, block length, and the number of denoising steps. Instead of committing permanently to each left-to-right token, the model can update a block of positions using broader sequence context. This may concentrate probability mass around coherent low-budget solutions, but it may also reduce the diversity gained from repeated sampling.

\subsection{Models Under Study}
LLaDA and Dream fit the masked diffusion family. Their generation is controlled through generation length, diffusion steps, and block length. Qwen2.5 and Llama 3.1 fit the autoregressive family. Their main pass@k runs use vLLM sampling with generation length and temperature, but no diffusion block or denoising schedule.

The thesis hypothesis is therefore: diffusion decoding may concentrate probability mass around coherent low-budget solutions, while autoregressive temperature sampling may explore a broader candidate set and therefore benefit more from large-$k$ pass@k. The results support this pattern clearly on Countdown-cd4 and partially on Trip Planning, while GSM8K is saturated at high $k$ and mainly reveals prompt and parser sensitivity.
