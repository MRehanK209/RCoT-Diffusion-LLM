\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Parser dependence.} GSM8K requires boxed final answers in the main evaluator, Countdown requires a chained-equation format, and Trip Planning requires parseable itinerary lines. An alternate fallback parser would rescue $3.24$ percentage points for LLaDA and $4.03$ percentage points for Qwen on GSM8K instruct without changing the canonical thesis numbers. On Trip Planning, more than $50\%$ of base-mode samples fail the canonical parser. Absolute scores are therefore a joint property of model and parser, and cross-paradigm rankings should be read together with the parser-failure summary in Section~\ref{sec:parser-results}.

\paragraph{Benchmark saturation and ceiling.} GSM8K is partly saturated at this scale: all four model families reach pass@128 above $97\%$ in the main templated runs, and three of the four reach $100\%$. The benchmark therefore answers prompt-mode and parser-sensitivity questions well but does not separate paradigms at large $k$. AIME, MATH-Beyond, or LiveCodeBench would provide more useful headroom and are the natural next benchmarks for a follow-up study.

\paragraph{Backend and inference asymmetry.} Diffusion pass@k uses fast-dLLM and autoregressive pass@k uses vLLM. The two backends differ in batching, KV-cache reuse, and serving overhead, so wall-time speedup numbers reported in the literature are not directly comparable to the ones in this thesis. Section~\ref{sec:results-hyperparameters} reports a compute-equalized GSM8K comparison, but a controlled head-to-head latency study at fixed batch size and identical hardware is left for future work.

\paragraph{Sample-budget and seed coverage.} Pass@k is computed at $128$ samples for GSM8K and Countdown but only $64$ samples for Trip Planning. Comparisons across benchmarks therefore mix sample budgets, and the pass@128 reversal on Countdown cannot be confirmed at pass@128 on Trip Planning from the existing data. Statistical significance and bootstrap confidence intervals are not reported; a single seed with $n=128$ is a partial substitute for repeated runs and only justifies claims about effects of similar magnitude or larger.

\paragraph{Model scale and family coverage.} The comparison is restricted to $7$B--$8$B checkpoints from four model families. No claim about how the pass@k ranking reversal or the per-question correlation pattern extrapolates to $70$B$+$ AR models or to next-generation diffusion language models is supported by this data.

\paragraph{Oracle gap is an upper bound.} The complementarity claim is supported by Pearson correlations and oracle unions over existing samples. No deployable ensemble or selective-routing experiment is implemented, and the gain achievable by any practical selector is necessarily smaller than the oracle ceiling reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-correlation}.
