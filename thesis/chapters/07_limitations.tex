\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Model, benchmark, and external validity.}
The comparison is restricted to four open model families in the $7$B--$8$B range and to three benchmarks: GSM8K, Countdown-cd4, and Trip Planning. It therefore cannot support claims about much larger AR systems, future diffusion LLMs, proprietary models, multimodal systems, or tasks outside the evaluated reasoning and planning settings. GSM8K is also partly saturated at high $k$, so it is more informative about prompting and parser behavior than about high-budget paradigm separation. The observed ranking reversal is strongest on Countdown and visible but less clean on Trip Planning; it should not be generalized to all math, code, or planning tasks without additional non-saturated benchmarks.

\paragraph{Measurement and statistical validity.}
The reported scores depend on task-specific parsers as well as model outputs. GSM8K requires boxed final answers in the canonical evaluator, Countdown requires valid chained equations, and Trip Planning requires parseable itinerary structure. This means absolute scores partly reflect output-format compliance, especially on Trip Planning. The pass@k estimates are also finite-sample estimates: GSM8K and Countdown use up to $128$ samples per question, while Trip Planning uses up to $64$. The study does not report bootstrap confidence intervals, paired significance tests, or repeated sampling seeds, so close differences should not be overinterpreted. This limits claims about small margins, but not the broader observation that rankings change with sampling budget, prompting, parser behavior, and evaluation protocol.

\paragraph{Systems and oracle validity.}
The inference backends are not directly comparable: diffusion runs use fast-dLLM, while AR runs use vLLM, with different batching, cache behavior, denoising schedules, and serving overhead. The paper therefore should not be read as a controlled wall-clock efficiency study. Similarly, the oracle-union analysis is an upper bound over existing samples, not an implemented ensemble. A deployable hybrid system would require held-out routing or selection, confidence calibration across paradigms, parser-aware validation, and cost-aware sampling. The oracle gains show that complementary correct samples exist; they do not show that a practical selector can recover the same gains.
