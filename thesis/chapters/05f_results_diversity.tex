\subsection{Sampling and pass@k growth}
\label{sec:results-diversity}

The pass@k analyses show how much each model benefits from additional samples. Normalized-answer diversity provides a second view of the same candidate sets: it counts how many distinct parsed answers appear across samples, rather than whether any one of them is correct. Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-gain} compares this diversity measure with the gain from pass@1 to the largest sampled $k$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/diversity_vs_passk_gain.pdf}
\caption{Mean normalized-answer diversity versus pass@k gain across the main comparison conditions. The figure reports an empirical association in the sampled outputs, not a causal mechanism.}
\label{fig:diversity-gain}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Concrete example.} A representative Countdown-cd4 base question with target $39$ and inputs $\{23, 10, 3, 28\}$ illustrates measured candidate-set variation. LLaDA-Base produces one correct normalized chain, \texttt{23+10=33,33/3=11,11+28=39}, in $6$ of its $128$ samples. Qwen-Base produces $94$ distinct first-line candidate chains on the same question, although none is correct for this instance. The example therefore illustrates candidate spread rather than an AR success on that item; across the full $992$-question set, the larger measured AR candidate set is consistent with steeper pass@k growth under the tested sampling setup. Additional traces, including paradigm-specific failure cases, are reproduced in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-qualitative}.

Countdown base is the clearest case: LLaDA averages $1.31$ unique normalized answers per question and a $12.93$-point pass@k gain, while Qwen averages $44.65$ unique normalized answers and a $47.27$-point gain. Llama-Base has the largest measured normalized-answer count in this condition, with $58.84$ unique normalized answers per question and a $46.80$-point gain. Trip Planning is noisier because parser validity interacts with candidate variation, but Llama obtains the largest high-$k$ gain in both base and instruct Trip Planning conditions. The pattern is best read as a property of these sampled runs, not as a general statement about all AR or diffusion models.
