\subsection{RQ2: Sampling diversity and pass@k growth}
RQ2 can be reframed as a question about search behavior: which models turn additional samples into genuinely different valid candidates? Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-gain} and Table~\ref{tab:diversity-summary} show that the planning benchmarks are at least consistent with the concentration-versus-diversity interpretation. Diffusion models often produce fewer distinct normalized answers and smaller pass@k gains, while the strongest autoregressive high-$k$ models maintain far broader output diversity and convert it into steeper pass@k growth.

These metrics do not prove the internal mechanism of diffusion denoising. They do, however, make the thesis interpretation more disciplined. Countdown Base is the clearest case: LLaDA averages only 1.31 unique normalized answers per question and a 12.93-point pass@k gain, while Qwen averages 44.65 unique normalized answers and a 47.27-point gain. Trip Planning is noisier because parser-validity interacts with diversity, but the same broad pattern remains visible.
