\subsection{Sampling diversity and pass@k growth}
\label{sec:results-diversity}

The pass@k ranking reversal can be reframed as a question about search behavior: which models turn additional samples into genuinely different valid candidates? Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-gain} shows that the planning benchmarks are at least consistent with the concentration-versus-diversity reading. Diffusion models often produce fewer distinct normalized answers and smaller pass@k gains, while the strongest autoregressive high-$k$ models maintain far broader output diversity and convert it into steeper pass@k growth.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/diversity_vs_passk_gain.pdf}
\caption{Mean answer diversity versus pass@k gain across all main comparison conditions. The planning-benchmark points are consistent with a concentration-versus-diversity trade-off: broader answer diversity tends to accompany larger gains from search.}
\label{fig:diversity-gain}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Concrete example.} A representative Countdown-cd4 base question with target $39$ and inputs $\{23, 10, 3, 28\}$ illustrates the duplicate-rate gap. LLaDA-Base produces the same valid expression \texttt{23+10=33,33/3=11,11+28=39} on the majority of its $128$ samples, while Qwen-Base produces $40$+ syntactically distinct candidate chains on the same question. The diffusion answer is correct on this question but contributes a single solution to its pass@k; the autoregressive model converts its broader spread into steeper pass@k growth across the full $992$-question set. Additional traces, including paradigm-specific failure cases, are reproduced in Appendix~A.

These metrics do not prove the internal mechanism of diffusion denoising. They do, however, make the thesis interpretation more disciplined. Countdown base is the clearest case: LLaDA averages only $1.31$ unique normalized answers per question and a $12.93$-point pass@k gain, while Qwen averages $44.65$ unique normalized answers and a $47.27$-point gain. Llama-Base is even broader, with $58.84$ unique normalized answers per question and a $46.80$-point gain. Trip Planning is noisier because parser validity interacts with diversity, but the same broad pattern remains visible: Llama obtains the largest high-$k$ gain in both base and instruct Trip Planning conditions.
