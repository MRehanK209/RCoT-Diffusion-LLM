\subsection{Parser sensitivity and failure rates}
\label{sec:parser-results}

The parser affects the measured scores differently across benchmarks. GSM8K and Countdown rarely fail at the parser-entry stage, so their main errors are wrong answers, invalid equation chains, or missing required answer formatting. Trip Planning is different: many outputs are rejected before semantic scoring because the itinerary structure is malformed. For that benchmark, accuracy reflects both planning quality and compliance with the parser's expected format.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/parser_failure_rates.pdf}
\caption{Canonical parser failure rates for the six main comparison conditions. GSM8K and Countdown almost never fail at the parser-entry stage, while Trip Planning can be dominated by malformed outputs.}
\label{fig:parser-failure-rates}
\end{figure}

The sensitivity analysis is deliberately kept separate from the canonical results. GSM8K parser crashes are rare in the main runs, but a more permissive final-number fallback would recover correct unboxed answers: in the instruct-templated $128$-sample run it rescues $3.24$ percentage points for LLaDA and $4.03$ points for Qwen. Countdown behaves differently: parser crashes are effectively absent, and an alternate single-expression parser does not rescue additional correct samples because the dominant failure is semantic invalidity, especially wrong number use. Trip Planning is the parser-heavy task. Base runs have malformed-plan rates above $47\%$ for both diffusion models and $57.8\%$ for Llama-Base; Llama-Instruct under the templated prompt still fails to produce parseable plans on $68.0\%$ of samples.
