\subsection{Parser sensitivity and failure rates}
\label{sec:parser-results}

The measured pass@k story depends on the parser layer, but the dependence is benchmark-specific. On GSM8K and Countdown, outright parser crashes are almost nonexistent; the more important issue is output-format compliance. On Trip Planning, malformed plans can dominate the error profile, especially for base prompts and for Llama-Instruct under the templated prompt. Figure~\ref{fig:parser-failure-rates} and Table~\ref{tab:parser-summary} summarize the main rates.

The sensitivity analysis is deliberately kept separate from the canonical thesis numbers. On GSM8K, a more permissive fallback parser recovers correct unboxed answers for all four models, but the effect is largest for the autoregressive checkpoints: Qwen gains $229$ additional correct samples in the base $128$-sample run and $661$ in the instruct-templated run, while LLaDA gains $531$ in the instruct-templated run. On Countdown, by contrast, an alternate single-expression parser does not rescue any additional correct samples from the canonical files; the main barrier is semantic invalidity, not chain parsing. On Trip Planning, the dominant parser effect is whether the model emits the required itinerary scaffold at all.
