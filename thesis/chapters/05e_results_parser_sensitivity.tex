\subsection{Parser sensitivity and failure rates}
\label{sec:parser-results}

The measured pass@k story depends on the parser layer, but the dependence is benchmark-specific. On GSM8K and Countdown, outright parser crashes are almost nonexistent; the more important issue is output-format compliance. On Trip Planning, malformed plans can dominate the error profile, especially for base prompts and for Llama-Instruct under the templated prompt. Figure~\ref{fig:parser-failure-rates} summarizes the main rates.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/parser_failure_rates.pdf}
\caption{Canonical parser failure rates for the six main comparison conditions. GSM8K and Countdown almost never fail at the parser-entry stage, while Trip Planning can be dominated by malformed outputs.}
\label{fig:parser-failure-rates}
\end{figure}

The sensitivity analysis is deliberately kept separate from the canonical results. GSM8K parser crashes are below $0.1\%$, but a more permissive final-number fallback would recover correct unboxed answers: in the instruct-templated $128$-sample run it rescues $3.24$ percentage points for LLaDA and $4.03$ points for Qwen. Countdown behaves differently: parser crashes are effectively absent, and an alternate single-expression parser does not rescue additional correct samples because the dominant failure is semantic invalidity, especially wrong number use. Trip Planning is the parser-heavy task. Base runs have malformed-plan rates above $47\%$ for both diffusion models and $57.8\%$ for Llama-Base; Llama-Instruct under the templated prompt still fails to produce parseable plans on $68.0\%$ of samples.
