\subsection{Per-question correlation and paradigm complementarity}
\label{sec:results-correlation}

The pass@k results compare aggregate accuracies. Per-question accuracy vectors carry a finer signal: for each model, the fraction of $n$ samples that solve a given question. Pearson correlations between these vectors quantify how strongly two models share failure modes. Figure~\ref{fig:correlation-summary} reports the within-paradigm and strongest cross-paradigm correlation for each of the six main conditions; the full pairwise matrices are reproduced in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-correlation}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/correlation_summary.pdf}
\caption{Within-paradigm and strongest cross-paradigm Pearson correlations across the six main comparison conditions. Countdown Base has the strongest dLLM--dLLM agreement, while Trip Planning has the strongest AR--AR agreement.}
\label{fig:correlation-summary}
\end{figure}

High within-paradigm correlations indicate that models from the same generation paradigm tend to share failure modes. Countdown Base is the most informative case: LLaDA-Base and Dream-Base correlate at $r=0.638$, which exceeds Dream-Base--Qwen-Base at $r=0.572$ even though Dream shares Qwen's pretrained lineage. This is evidence of behavioral alignment by generation paradigm beyond pretrained lineage, not proof of a mechanistic cause. Trip Planning Instruct adds a complementary observation: AR--AR correlation is high while cross-paradigm correlations are lower, especially Dream-Instruct--Qwen-Instruct at $r=0.157$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/oracle_ensemble_gain.pdf}
\caption{Oracle complementarity gain over the best single model. GSM8K is saturated, but both planning benchmarks show measurable paradigm-level union gains.}
\label{fig:oracle-gain}
\end{figure}

The oracle analysis follows from the correlation question: if models solve different questions, a perfect selector could combine them. The reported oracle unions are therefore upper bounds on complementarity from the existing samples, not practical methods. On Countdown base, the best single model solves $509$ of $992$ questions, while a Dream$+$Qwen oracle union solves $541$ and the full four-model union solves $604$. On Trip Planning base, the best single model solves $51$ of $200$ questions, while Dream$+$Llama solves $64$ and the full four-model union solves $71$. A deployable system would need a learned selector, verifier, router, or parser-aware validation method.
