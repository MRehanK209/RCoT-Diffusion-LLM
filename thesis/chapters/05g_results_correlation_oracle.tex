\subsection{Per-question correlation and oracle complementarity}
\label{sec:results-correlation}

The pass@k results compare aggregate accuracies. Per-question accuracy vectors carry a finer signal: for each model, the fraction of $n$ samples that solve a given question. Pearson correlations between these vectors quantify how strongly two models share failure modes. Figure~\ref{fig:correlation-summary} reports the within-group and strongest cross-group correlation for each of the six main conditions; the full pairwise matrices are reproduced in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-correlation}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/correlation_summary.pdf}
\caption{Within-group and strongest cross-group correlations.}
\label{fig:correlation-summary}
\end{figure}

High within-group correlations indicate that some evaluated configurations share failure modes. Countdown Base is the most informative case: LLaDA-Base and Dream-Base correlate at $r=0.638$, which exceeds Dream-Base--Qwen-Base at $r=0.572$ even though Dream shares Qwen's pretrained lineage. This pattern is consistent with shared behavior among the evaluated diffusion decoding setups, but it should not be read as proof that the generation interface alone determines the correlation structure. Trip Planning Instruct adds a related observation: AR--AR correlation is high while cross-group correlations are lower, especially Dream-Instruct--Qwen-Instruct at $r=0.157$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\textwidth]{images/oracle_ensemble_gain.pdf}
\caption{Oracle union gain over the best single model.}
\label{fig:oracle-gain}
\end{figure}

The oracle analysis estimates an upper bound on complementarity by asking how many questions are solved by at least one model in a set. This is not a practical ensemble method, because it assumes knowledge of correctness at selection time. It is useful only as an upper bound on complementarity in the existing samples. On Countdown base, the best single model solves $509$ of $992$ questions, while a Dream$+$Qwen oracle union solves $541$ and the full four-model union solves $604$. On Trip Planning base, the best single model solves $51$ of $200$ questions, while Dream$+$Llama solves $64$ and the full four-model union solves $71$. A practical system would need a learned selector, verifier, router, or parser-aware validation method.
