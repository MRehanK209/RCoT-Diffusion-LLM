The key result is Countdown Base. LLaDA-Base and Dream-Base correlate at $r=0.6382$, which is higher than Dream-Base and Qwen-Base at $r=0.5718$ even though Dream shares Qwen lineage. This suggests that, for this constrained planning task, generation paradigm is more predictive of failure-mode alignment than shared pretrained architecture. Trip Planning Instruct adds a different insight: cross-paradigm correlations are low, especially Dream-Instruct versus Qwen-Instruct at $r=0.1572$, which points to complementarity and possible ensemble value.

The oracle analysis makes the complementarity claim more concrete. On Countdown Base, the best single model solves 509 of 992 questions, but a Dream+Qwen oracle union solves 541 and the full four-model union solves 604. Trip Planning is even more striking relative to its scale: the best single model solves 51 of 200 base questions, while Dream+Llama solves 64 and the full four-model union solves 71. These are only upper bounds because no practical selector is implemented, but they show that the correlation results are not merely statistical decoration.
