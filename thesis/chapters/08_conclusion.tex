\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis does not show that diffusion language models universally dominate autoregressive language models. It shows that the comparison is protocol-dependent. After a diffusion hyperparameter sweep that characterized generation length, block length, and denoising-step behavior, the main benchmark results show a consistent trade-off on planning-oriented tasks: diffusion models are stronger in deterministic or low-$k$ settings, while autoregressive models gain more from stochastic sampling and can overtake at high pass@k. GSM8K is saturated at high $k$ and is therefore most useful here as a prompt-format and parser-sensitivity check rather than as decisive paradigm-ranking evidence.

The per-question analyses support a complementary view of the two paradigms. Correlations indicate that AR and diffusion models solve overlapping but non-identical subsets of questions, and the Countdown Base result suggests that generation paradigm can shape failure-mode alignment beyond pretrained lineage. Oracle unions make this complementarity visible as an upper bound, but they are not deployable systems. A practical continuation of this work is therefore not to replace AR models with diffusion models, but to study hybrid or selective-routing methods that combine low-budget diffusion candidates with broader AR sampling, verifier-based selection, or parser-aware repair.
