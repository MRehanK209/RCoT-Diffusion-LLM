\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis compared diffusion and autoregressive language models on reasoning and planning tasks under controlled evaluation conditions. The results do not support a universal claim that one generation paradigm dominates the other. They show a budget-sensitive trade-off. Diffusion models are competitive or leading in several deterministic and low-$k$ planning settings, while autoregressive models can gain more from repeated stochastic sampling and overtake at high pass@k. GSM8K is largely saturated at high $k$ and is most useful here as a diagnostic for prompt format and parser behavior.

The per-question analyses show that the paradigms solve overlapping but non-identical subsets of problems. This makes complementarity a more useful conclusion than replacement. As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:results-correlation}, oracle unions make this complementarity visible only as a diagnostic over sampled outputs. The next step is therefore to study practical hybrid systems: diffusion or autoregressive candidate generation combined with learned verification, routing, selection, or parser-aware repair.
