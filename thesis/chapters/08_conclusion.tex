\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The thesis answers its motivating question conditionally. Three findings carry the result. First, on planning benchmarks, diffusion language models lead at deterministic and low-$k$ evaluation: LLaDA-Base reaches Countdown pass@1 of $0.143$ against Qwen-Base $0.040$, and Dream-Base leads Trip Planning at every $k\le 16$. Second, autoregressive models overtake at high sampling budgets: Qwen-Base reaches Countdown pass@128 of $0.513$ versus Dream-Base $0.376$ and LLaDA-Base $0.272$. The ranking that the evaluator reports therefore depends on the sampling budget, not just on the models. Third, per-question correlation analysis shows that diffusion models share failure modes with one another more than they share with the autoregressive model whose backbone Dream inherits ($r=0.638$ for LLaDA--Dream versus $r=0.572$ for Dream--Qwen on Countdown base). Generation paradigm, not pretrained architecture, is the stronger predictor of which questions a model fails on this constrained planning benchmark.

The strongest practical implication is that the diffusion and autoregressive paradigms are complementary rather than competing. On Trip Planning instruct, cross-paradigm correlations fall in $[0.16, 0.25]$ and a Dream$+$Llama oracle union improves over the best single model by $6.5$ percentage points. A controlled study of practical ensemble or selective-routing methods that recovers a meaningful fraction of this oracle ceiling is the most direct continuation of this work.
