\section{Qualitative Examples}
\label{sec:appendix-qualitative}
The following compact examples are extracted from the thesis generation JSON files. Long inputs and outputs are wrapped using \texttt{\textbackslash seqsplit} so that they break cleanly across lines.

\subsection{Countdown-cd4 Base}
The Countdown examples show raw continuations from the generation files. When a completion continues into the next prompt with an \texttt{Input:} suffix, the parser evaluates the leading comma-separated equation chain.

\paragraph{dLLM-only solved.}
\textbf{Question.} \seqsplit{23,10,3,28,39}\\
\textbf{LLaDA.} \seqsplit{23+10=33,33/3=11,11+28=39 Input: 60}\\
\textbf{Qwen.} \seqsplit{23-10=13,3*28=84,13+84=97,39*2=7}

\paragraph{AR-only solved.}
\textbf{Question.} \seqsplit{97,2,59,5,14}\\
\textbf{Qwen.} \seqsplit{97-59=38,38/2=19,19-5=14 Input: 80,4}\\
\textbf{LLaDA.} \seqsplit{97/2=48,59/5=34,48-34=14 Input: 97,}

\paragraph{Both paradigms solved.}
\textbf{Question.} \seqsplit{57,61,22,15,41}\\
\textbf{Dream.} \seqsplit{61-57=4,22+4=26,26+15=41 Input: 99,5}\\
\textbf{Qwen.} \seqsplit{61+22=83,57-15=42,83-42=41 Input: 60}

\paragraph{Neither paradigm solved.}
\textbf{Question.} \seqsplit{2,52,20,73,57}\\
\textbf{LLaDA.} \seqsplit{52-2=50,73-20=53,50+53=57 Input: 5,} (invalid arithmetic: $50+53$ is not $57$).\\
\textbf{Qwen.} \seqsplit{52-2=50,73-20=53,50+53=103 Input: 79}

\subsection{GSM8K parser-sensitivity example}

\textbf{Question.} ``Sara wants to buy a new jacket and 2 pairs of shoes. The jacket costs \$30 and each pair of shoes \$20. Sara babysits 4 times at \$5 each \ldots''\\
\textbf{Raw sample.} ``\textit{First, we need to determine how much money Sara earns from babysitting. She babysits 4 times, earning \$5 each time, so she earns $4 \times 5 = \$20$ from babysitting. \ldots}'' (no boxed final answer, so the canonical scorer marks this sample wrong; an alternate fallback parser would extract the numeric answer).

\subsection{Countdown parser-failure example}

\textbf{Question.} \seqsplit{97,2,59,5,14}\\
\textbf{Raw sample.} \seqsplit{97/2=48,59/5=34,48-34=14 Input: 97,}\\
The parser first extracts the leading equation chain. Although the final expression ends at $14$, the chain is rejected because the intermediate equations are arithmetically invalid under exact evaluation: $97/2$ is not $48$ and $59/5$ is not $34$.

\subsection{Trip Planning parser-failure example}

\textbf{Question.} \seqsplit{Riga**Hamburg**Bucharest**Naples**Nice**Reykjavik**Prague**Florence**Madrid**Seville||2**4**2**4**4**3**2**4**5**4}\\
\textbf{Raw sample.} ``Trip plan for 10 European cities, 25 days. Day 1--5: arriving in Hamburg and visit Hamburg for 4 days. Day 5: fly Hamburg $\to$ Madrid \ldots''\\
The plan starts in the wrong city and is therefore rejected; the model's output is parseable but semantically incorrect.
