\section*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This thesis investigates whether chain-of-thought reasoning and planning can be rethought through diffusion language models. It compares LLaDA-8B and Dream-v0-7B with Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B across GSM8K, Countdown-cd4, and Trip Planning. The evaluation combines deterministic accuracy, stochastic pass@k, parser-controlled answer extraction, prompt-mode ablations, and per-question accuracy analysis. The central finding is that diffusion language models are stronger in deterministic and low-k planning settings, while autoregressive models improve more under large-k sampling and can overtake at higher sample budgets. Prompt format and few-shot examples substantially affect the ranking, and per-question correlations show that the paradigms solve overlapping but meaningfully different subsets of problems. Diffusion language models therefore do not simply replace autoregressive models; they change the evaluation trade-off and motivate hybrid or ensemble-style reasoning evaluation.
