\section*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This first thesis draft investigates whether chain-of-thought reasoning can be rethought through diffusion language models and, if so, under which evaluation protocol such a comparison is most meaningful. The empirical study compares two diffusion families, LLaDA-8B and Dream-v0-7B, with two autoregressive families, Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, across GSM8K, Countdown-cd4, and Trip Planning under deterministic and stochastic pass@k evaluation. The current evidence supports three provisional contributions: diffusion checkpoints are strongest in deterministic or pass@1 planning settings, autoregressive checkpoints overtake at larger k; per-question Pearson correlations indicate that failure-mode alignment is often organized more strongly by generation paradigm than by shared pretrained architecture; and prompt sensitivity differs by paradigm, with diffusion models stronger at zero-shot GSM8K while autoregressive models benefit more from few-shot examples.
