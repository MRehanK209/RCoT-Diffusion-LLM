\section*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
Reasoning benchmarks often reduce a model to one generated answer, even though many applications can sample more than once. This thesis is motivated by recent large-$k$ reasoning work showing that top-1 scores and pass@k can reward different properties of the sampled candidate set. It asks whether masked diffusion language models show a similar large-$k$ coverage effect when compared with autoregressive language models. The surrounding analyses of low-budget rankings, parser behavior, and per-question overlap are used to make that question measurable and interpretable.

The experiments compare LLaDA, Dream, Qwen2.5, and Llama~3.1 on GSM8K, Countdown-cd4, and Trip Planning with shared downstream parsing and scoring. The main pattern depends on sampling budget rather than giving a universal winner. On Countdown-cd4, LLaDA-Base and Dream-Base have the strongest estimated stochastic pass@1 scores, but Qwen-Base and Llama-Base overtake them by pass@128. On Trip Planning, Dream is strongest in deterministic and low-budget settings, while a flat-prompt Llama-Instruct run gives the best observed high-budget result. GSM8K is close to saturated at high $k$, so it mainly exposes prompt-format and answer-extraction sensitivity.

The diffusion runs do not show the same large-$k$ coverage advantage that motivated the study. Instead, the evidence points to a practical trade-off: LLaDA and Dream can be competitive when only a few candidates are available, while Qwen and Llama benefit more from repeated stochastic sampling in several settings. Per-question analyses show that the success sets overlap but are not identical, which supports complementarity rather than replacement. The natural next step is to combine candidate generation with verification, routing, selection, or parser-aware repair.
