\section*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This thesis draft investigates whether chain-of-thought reasoning and planning can be rethought through diffusion language models. It compares LLaDA-8B and Dream-v0-7B with Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B across GSM8K, Countdown-cd4, and Trip Planning. The evaluation combines deterministic accuracy, stochastic pass@k, parser-controlled answer extraction, prompt-mode ablations, and per-question correlation analysis. The central finding is that diffusion language models are stronger in deterministic and low-k planning settings, while autoregressive models improve more under large-k sampling and can overtake at higher sample budgets. Prompt format and few-shot examples substantially affect the ranking, and per-question correlations suggest that the paradigms solve overlapping but meaningfully different subsets of problems. The resulting conclusion is not that diffusion language models simply replace autoregressive models, but that they change the evaluation trade-off and motivate hybrid or ensemble-style reasoning evaluation.
